Timbuktu Manuscripts & Ahmad Baba

Timbuktu was a centre of Islamic learning; Ahmad Baba (1556–1627) exemplified its scholarly tradition.

Centre of learning

From the 14th–17th centuries, Timbuktu attracted scholars and students across the Sahara. Its madrasas, libraries and book trade produced and preserved vast manuscript collections on law, theology, astronomy, medicine, history and literature.

Ahmad Baba al-Timbukti

Ahmad Baba (1556–1627) was a prominent Malian jurist and scholar. His works on Islamic law and critiques of unlawful enslavement remain influential. He represented the rigorous scholarship that characterised Timbuktu's intellectual milieu.

Manuscript heritage

Tens of thousands of manuscripts survive in family and institutional collections. Efforts to digitise and preserve them have highlighted the depth of African written knowledge traditions.
