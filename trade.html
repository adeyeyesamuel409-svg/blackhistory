Trade that spanned three continents

African gold and salt moved along routes longer than any in Europe. Before Columbus, caravans crossed the Sahara to the Mediterranean, and dhows rode the monsoon winds to Arabia, Persia, India and China.

Beyond the map

How the networks worked

Sahara

Gold & salt, the engines of the desert

West African gold flowed north; Saharan salt flowed south, cut into slabs and traded for its weight in gold. Caravans of many thousands of camels linked Timbuktu and Gao to Sijilmasa, Tunis and Cairo.

Learning

Timbuktu: a city of books

Timbuktu's scholars copied and traded manuscripts in law, astronomy, medicine and grammar. Families guarded private libraries for centuries — tens of thousands of manuscripts survive today.

Indian Ocean

The Swahili world & the monsoon

Dhow captains timed voyages to the monsoon winds, linking Kilwa, Mombasa, Malindi and Sofala to Arabia, Persia, India and China. Swahili cities minted coins and built in coral stone and lime.

Testimony

A world in motion

Travellers like Ibn Battuta (Mali, 1352–53) and al-Bakri left eyewitness accounts of African courts, markets and kings — African societies described from the inside, before European expansion.
