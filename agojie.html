The Agojie of Dahomey

All-female military regiments of the Kingdom of Dahomey (present-day Benin), documented by 19th-century eyewitnesses including European visitors.

Historical evidence

The Agojie (often called "Dahomey Amazons") are attested by 19th-century observers. While sensationalised in popular culture, contemporary accounts confirm their existence as organised regiments under the Dahomean monarchy.

Organisation

Agojie regiments formed part of Dahomey's standing forces. They were trained, armed and deployed in campaigns. Accounts describe discipline, initiation and royal service, though exact numbers vary between sources.

Contemporary eyewitnesses

19th-century visitors such as Richard F. Burton, Sir Richard Burton's contemporaries, and accounts from the Anglo-Dahomey Wars (e.g. Forbes, Skertchly) document their presence. Later critical histories separate verifiable facts from colonial and popular exaggerations.

Sources

  • Robin Law, The Slave Coast of West Africa, 1550–1750 (Oxford, 1991).
  • Edna G. Bay, Wives of the Leopard: Gender, Politics, and Culture in the Kingdom of Dahomey (Virginia, 1998).
  • Stanley B. Alpern, Amazons of Black Sparta: The Women Warriors of Dahomey (New York, 1998).
