Yaa Asantewaa

Asante queen mother of Edweso who led the War of the Golden Stool against British colonial rule in 1900.

Context

Following the 1896 exile of Asantehene Prempeh I and other leaders, the British sought greater control over Asante. The Golden Stool (Sika Dwa Kofi) remained the symbol of Asante sovereignty and unity.

War of the Golden Stool (1900)

When British governor Frederick Hodgson demanded the Golden Stool, Yaa Asantewaa rallied the Asante Confederacy. She famously declared that if the men would not fight, the women would. The ensuing war (March–September 1900) saw fierce resistance before eventual British victory and Yaa Asantewaa's exile.

Legacy

Yaa Asantewaa became a symbol of female leadership and anti-colonial resistance. Ghana's Yaa Asantewaa Museum in Kumasi and her continued celebration reflect her enduring legacy.

Sources

  • Ivor Wilks, Asante in the Nineteenth Century (Cambridge, 1975).
  • David Owusu-Ansah, Historical Dictionary of Ghana (Scarecrow, 2014).
