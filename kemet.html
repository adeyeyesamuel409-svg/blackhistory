Pillar 02 · Kemet & Nubia

Built by African engineers

The pyramids of Giza and Meroë were the work of indigenous African architects, surveyors and organised labour — not slaves, and not mystery. Here is the evidence, and a lab to test it yourself.

Engineering lab

Move a 2.5-tonne block

Adjust water on the sand and the ramp angle, then set your workforce. Experiments (Fall et al., 2014) show that the right amount of water roughly halves the friction — the technique shown in the tomb of Djehutihotep.

Language, number & medicine

A civilisation that wrote it down

Writing

The scribe & the written word

Hieroglyphs, hieratic and later demotic recorded taxes, law, ritual and letters. The Rosetta Stone's trilingual decree (196 BCE) let Champollion crack the script in 1822 — an African writing system unlocking its own history.

Mathematics

Numbers for building & taxes

The Rhind Mathematical Papyrus (c. 1650 BCE) works problems in fractions, geometry, slope (seked) and volume — practical mathematics for surveying land and levelling pyramids, not abstract puzzles.

Medicine

Medicine of the body

Egyptian healers set bones, treated wounds and recorded remedies in the Ebers and Edwin Smith papyri — the latter a surgical treatise that describes the brain and treats injuries case by case with startling care.

Nubian pharaohs

Kush rules Egypt

From c. 744 BCE the kings of Kush — Piye, Shabaka, Taharqa — reunified Egypt as its 25th Dynasty, ruling from the Nile Delta to the confluence of the Atbara. Two African civilisations, one crown.
