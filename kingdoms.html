Pillar 03 · Kingdoms

Empires of a continent

Long before European contact, African states commanded the world's great trade routes and built centres of science and scholarship. Territories here are shown as approximate extents, not precise borders.

Wealth, art & faith

States of gold, bronze & letters

Mali

Gold of the Sahel

Mali's mansas controlled the trans-Saharan gold and salt routes. Mansa Musa's 1324 pilgrimage to Mecca was so lavish that European mapmakers drew him on the Catalan Atlas (1375), holding a nugget of gold.

Ife & Benin

Art as statecraft

The naturalistic brass heads of Ife and the cast-bronze plaques of Benin were court art of the highest order. Benin's guilds cast commemorative heads of the obas and the leopard figures that guarded the palace.

Kongo

Kongo & the Atlantic world

The Kingdom of Kongo traded and, from the 1490s, engaged with Portugal on its own terms. Its nkisi nkondi figures — “power figures” driven through with nails to seal oaths and contracts — embody law, healing and the sacred.

Aksum & Ethiopia

Coins, script & a lasting church

Aksum minted its own gold coinage and adopted Christianity in the 4th century under King Ezana, in the Ge'ez script that Ethiopia still writes today. Illuminated Gospels preserved that literate faith through the centuries.
