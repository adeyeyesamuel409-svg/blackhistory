Nanny of the Maroons

Akan-born leader who established and defended Windward Maroon communities in Jamaica, culminating in treaty recognition of Maroon autonomy.

Historical context

After the English seized Jamaica from Spain in 1655, large numbers of enslaved Africans escaped and joined existing free Black communities (Maroons). By the early 18th century, the Windward Maroons under Nanny had become a formidable force in the eastern mountains.

Life & leadership

Oral tradition identifies Nanny as an Akan woman from the Gold Coast (present-day Ghana). She led military campaigns, organised provisioning and preserved cultural practices. Her guerrilla tactics leveraged mountain terrain to resist repeated colonial expeditions.

Treaty & legacy

Under pressure, the British signed treaties with Maroon leaders in 1738–1739. Nanny and the Windward Maroons secured terms that recognised their freedom and lands in return for peace and cooperation under certain conditions. Nanny is celebrated in Jamaica as a National Hero (posthumous recognition).
