The slave trades & the resistance

This is not one story but four — systems that spanned more than a thousand years. Told whole, with the numbers, the agency of the enslaved, and the resistance that never stopped.

A note before you read

This page describes the enslavement and trafficking of millions of African people across more than a thousand years. It includes one archival image made to expose the trade's inhumanity. It is told to honour the enslaved, their resistance and their descendants — to face the record honestly, not to sensationalise it. Please read with care.

Resistance

Resistance was constant

From refusal and flight to open revolt, the enslaved resisted at every stage. Maroon communities — escaped people who built free towns in the hills — fought colonial militias to a standstill in Jamaica, Suriname and Brazil.

Revolution

Haiti: the revolution that won

Beginning in 1791, the enslaved of Saint-Domingue rose under leaders including Toussaint Louverture. In 1804 they founded Haiti — the first modern republic born from a successful uprising of enslaved people, and a shock to every slave-holding power.

The Amistad

The Amistad, 1839

Kidnapped Mende people seized the slave ship Amistad and demanded a return to Africa. Their leader, Sengbe Pieh (Cinqué), argued his case to the U.S. Supreme Court — and won their freedom in 1841.

Voice

Voices that ended the trade

Survivors' testimony — Olaudah Equiano's narrative, the speeches of Frederick Douglass — put a human face to the trade and drove the abolition movement, alongside enslaved people who freed themselves.
