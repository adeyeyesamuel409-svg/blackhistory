Kilwa & the Swahili Coast

Stone-built city-states linking East Africa to the Indian Ocean world, with Kilwa rising to prominence from the 11th–15th centuries.

Rise of Kilwa

Kilwa Kisiwani (off modern Tanzania) grew wealthy by controlling trade in gold, ivory and other goods from the interior (notably Sofala). Its rulers minted distinctive Kilwa coinage and patronised mosques and palaces in coral stone.

Ibn Battuta's visit (1331)

Moroccan traveller Ibn Battuta visited Kilwa in 1331 and described it as one of the most beautiful and well-governed cities he had seen on the coast, noting its piety, justice and prosperity.

Swahili urbanism

Swahili city-states (Mogadishu, Mombasa, Zanzibar, Kilwa, Sofala) shared Islam, a maritime orientation, coral-stone architecture, and a Swahili language and identity shaped by African and Indian Ocean interactions.
